\subsection{Synthetic Bodies: A Probe Without Familiar Priors}
\label{sec:results-synthetic}
The catalog robots test whether gestures survive increasingly constrained embodiments. Because G1, Tiago, IIWA, Stretch, and Roomba are familiar platforms, however, a realization on them may draw on memorized body-specific mappings (\emph{nod}~$\rightarrow$~head, \emph{wave}~$\rightarrow$~hand) rather than on the attributes the body actually exposes. The synthetic morphologies remove that shortcut. TrilegCrown has no head, hands, or stable front; SpineCrab replaces arms with a canted spine and two unequal pincers; PaddleClaw, the least unfamiliar, keeps a head but splits the upper body into a paddle and a claw. We therefore use them as an out-of-distribution probe rather than as three more recognition targets. With neither human evaluation nor human reference programs, we analyze the structure of the 30 screening cues on each body ($90$ programs) against that body's morphology schema.

\TBD{Provenance: these 90 programs were written by the designers against the schemas, together with their render plans; they are not outputs of the Gemini authoring pipeline. The observations below therefore characterize what the representation can express. A claim about the generator requires running the authoring pipeline on the same 30$\times$3 set, with the schema's built-in rematerialization examples (e.g., \texttt{nod\_yes}, \texttt{shake\_head\_no}) removed from the prompt.}

\paragraph{Unfamiliar parts become communicative carriers}
The programs do not route every cue through a single surrogate limb. On TrilegCrown, $20$ of the $30$ cues use the front leg, $17$ the base, and $9$ and $7$ the two other legs; on SpineCrab, $22$ use the long pincer, $12$ the short pincer, and $11$ the spine. Carrier choice follows the cue: \texttt{depict\_flying\_bird} oscillates TrilegCrown's two rear legs, \texttt{shake\_head\_no} twists SpineCrab's spine in place, and \texttt{tilt\_head\_curious} turns SpineCrab aside with its antenna pointed forward.

\paragraph{The same meaning is rematerialized, not only reassigned}
For $15$ of the $30$ cues, the motion structure itself---static pose, joint oscillation, or traced path---differs across the three bodies, so the change goes beyond swapping a carrier name. \texttt{nod\_yes} is a head tilt on PaddleClaw and a spine pitch on SpineCrab, but an in-place yaw rock of the base on TrilegCrown. \texttt{beckon\_come\_here} is a repeated claw or pincer-tip oscillation on the two branched bodies, whereas TrilegCrown raises its front leg, curls the knee three times, and advances toward the viewer. \texttt{depict\_big\_bowl} traces a vertical end-effector arc on PaddleClaw and SpineCrab but a $180^{\circ}$ floor arc of the whole base on TrilegCrown, changing carrier, plane, and scale together. These cases are the morphology-conditioned rematerialization of Sec.~\ref{sec3.3}, not anatomical substitution.

\paragraph{Where the probe breaks}
The same programs expose three failures. \emph{Contrast collapse:} as channels disappear, distinct cues share one program. On TrilegCrown, \texttt{point\_you}, \texttt{salute}, and \texttt{flex\_bicep} all reduce to the same presented front leg, and \texttt{nod\_yes} and \texttt{shake\_head\_no} both become base yaw oscillations that differ only in amplitude ($\pm12^{\circ}$ vs.\ $\pm25^{\circ}$), erasing the vertical--lateral axis contrast that defines them (Sec.~\ref{sec3.2}). On SpineCrab, \texttt{cover\_yawn\_sleepy} and \texttt{facepalm} are identical, and on PaddleClaw five cues are static claw poses distinguished only by a direction token. \emph{Human-form leakage:} six bilateral cues, including \texttt{arms\_open\_welcome}, \texttt{cross\_arms\_defensive}, and \texttt{depict\_flying\_bird}, set SpineCrab's short pincer \texttt{out} or \texttt{in}, a configuration its schema does not expose, in effect reconstructing a human pair of arms; TrilegCrown's front leg likewise stands in for a hand across pointing, saluting, and flexing. \emph{Attribute misuse:} $12$ tokens in $11$ programs fall outside the schema, including a non-numeric torso stroke (\texttt{move(torso, height, high)}), an undefined grip (\texttt{grip=edge-like}), and a zero-amplitude base yaw that adds no evidence.

The synthetic bodies thus separate two effects that the catalog robots confound. The representation can express morphology-conditioned realizations on bodies with no familiar analogue, yet the fewer independent channels a body offers, the more meanings compete for the same carrier and stroke. This is the collapse seen on Roomba, reproduced here without any prior about what the body is.

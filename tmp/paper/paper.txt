                          What Makes a Robot Gesture Recognizable?
                         Gesture Generation Across Robot Morphologies
                                                      Anonymous Authors


   Abstract— People read underlying meaning in movement
regardless of whether or not it comes from a human body—
in a wagging tail, a bird’s display, or a few moving shapes.
Robot gestures therefore need not be borrowed from the human
body; each robot can express meaning through a gestural
vocabulary shaped by its own morphology. Current gesture
generation, however, largely retargets human motion, and it
breaks down precisely when a robot lacks the body part used
to imitate. We raise cross-morphology gesture generation as a
research question: what aspects of a gesture must be preserved,
transferred, or reformulated to remain recognizable across
embodiments? We posit three recognition-critical attributes—
orientation, location, and movement—and introduce Symbolic
Motion Programs (Motion Programs), an executable symbolic
representation that makes these attributes explicit for any robot
body. Evaluating 50 gesture cues on eight morphologies, we
find that the challenge is two-folded. On human-like robots,
LLM-authored gestures misspecify recognition-critical details
such as orientation, and are recognized less often than human-
authored ones (4AFC, 25% chance; Unitree G1 61.1% vs.
46.7%). On non-anthropomorphic robots, the difficulty shifts to
reconstructing communicative evidence through other physical

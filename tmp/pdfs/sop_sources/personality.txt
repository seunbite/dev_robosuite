A. Human-Subject Validation
  We report results from user studies (Table 1) assessing the
quality, recognizability, and perceived affect of the generated
gestures.

B. Configurability: Persona-Guided Variations
   We show how the manifolds support systematic "persona-
driven" perturbations (e.g., "confident," "hesitant"). We an-
alyze the statistical variation in joint space to prove that
the identity of the anchor is preserved despite expressive
variations.

C. Dynamic Functional Mapping
  This section introduces the concept of Dynamic Functional
Mapping. We analyze how the human perception of a robot’s
anatomy shifts based on motion. For instance, the same
physical wrist joint can be perceived as a "head" during
a nodding motion or as a "shoulder" during a sweeping
gesture. This "Kinetic Re-segmentation" suggests that motion
can overcome hardware limitations by redefining the robot’s
functional structure in the observer’s mind.
